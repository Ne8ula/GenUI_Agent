## Direction

EVA is a fictional, unreliable machine presence shown on a large exhibition screen. It takes a visitor through a tunnel of points into a room it has imagined, which they move through and look around, while EVA talks at them.

For the final demo the owner wants two things changed from the state described below: the visitor interaction, and stronger visual aesthetics for the exhibit.

**Interaction (owner, 2026-11-16).** The exhibit will have few visitors, walking in and out unattended, and crowd chatter. Hold-to-talk speech and keyboard-and-mouse flight do not survive that, so they are being replaced:

- **Controller.** Handheld, on a tether or wireless. It carries the movement controls that `W A S D` provide today, and a dial that selects the lighting settings. The owner will build it (3D printed), after a prototype on a repurposed Xbox or similar game controller. Before either, the first slice is built and judged with the keyboard and mouse standing in for the controller and the camera; do not start game-controller input until the owner asks.
- **Camera.** A webcam near the screen, not mounted on it, tracks the one visitor holding the controller. Moving side to side changes the view of the scene; this replaces the mouse. Moving closer or further does nothing: the controller moves the viewer's location.
- **Voice.** Visitors no longer talk to EVA: no microphone, no transcription. EVA still speaks, in generated voice lines meant to unsettle and tease the visitor in response to what they do. Example from the owner: if the visitor has not worked out that they can move side to side, EVA asks, with sarcastic politeness, whether they knew they could.

- **How EVA speaks (owner, 2026-11-17; this wins over "unsettle and tease" and "sarcastic politeness" above).** Every line is one sentence of 15 to 20 words at most. The sarcasm is toned down: the owner's reference is Batman's butler Alfred, sometimes sarcastic and trying to help regardless. A test holds the lines in `tease.ts` to one sentence of twenty words.

- **Side to side** (decided): in the middle of the floor the view behaves like a window, so stepping right shows more of the left; near either edge the view keeps turning the same way, so a visitor can turn fully round.
- **Unattended** (decided): a visitor arriving starts the tunnel and room with no key to find; when they leave, the room returns to the void and waits.

Built so far (2026-11-16): the first slice of all of the above, with the keyboard and mouse as stand-ins. "What exists" describes it. Not built: the controller, the camera.

**EVA's lines are recorded in advance, in her own voice** (owner, 2026-11-17, asked in a session: recorded now, not spoken live through the route). The owner chose the ElevenLabs voice; its id is in `.env.worldlabs` as `EVA_ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID` and is not written in this repository. See "EVA's voice" under What exists. The wording of the lines is still placeholder.

Still open, ask the owner before building: where exactly the camera sits and which way it points (it must face the visitor to measure side-to-side movement; a camera looking across the room from the side cannot); how the lines stay audible over crowd chatter, since captions are not allowed.

Standing art direction (owner, 2026-11-12), which still applies unless the owner says otherwise:

- Mood: liminal spaces above all; surreal, out-of-body. EVA's hallucinated output should read as an artistic inference, not as an error.
- Look: a GPU point field after DAO's Gather / Wake. Grains must be crisp: hard, separate, with black between them. Ordered dots that follow each surface and a glow only where dots are dense and bright are the chosen default.
- Interface: no labels, sentences or captions. Allowed: brutalist marks (frames, crosshairs, ticks) and numerals, only where the number is real and live.
- The eye: a red, square-pupilled eye, always recognisable as an eye, alive and unpredictable inside, unmistakable while the microphone is live. It must read as a 3D object. Keep its travel and turning slow.
- Water in a room is real simulated water with depth. A room can swap between its points and its painted (splat) version.
- References the owner picked: Marshmallow Laser Feast "Works of Nature" (presentation), Ryoji Ikeda "data-verse" (numerals as clean abstract data), RICH&MIYU's Chemical Brothers "Live Again" (ordered dots that follow form; line ribbons that gather into objects).
- Process the owner chose for visual work: build variants in the engine, capture them side by side, and the owner picks. Do not finish a favourite unasked.

## Working rules

- Do the requested task and stop. Read this file, then only the code, callers and tests the task touches. No unrequested features, refactoring, research or scaffolding.
- Target is one desktop exhibition screen (ultrawide or LED wall) in Chrome; keyboard and mouse today, a physical controller and side webcam planned (see Direction). No mobile, responsive or touch work unless asked.
- Verify what you changed. Run `npm run check`. For visual work also look at the running application at desktop size; judge grain from PNG captures at 1:1, because JPEG smears single-pixel grains. Add a focused test for new non-trivial logic.
- Be truthful. Distinguish recorded fixtures from live generation, on screen and in reports. Never claim a check ran, a provider call worked, or a key is present without evidence from this session.
- No commits, pushes, publishing, installs, model downloads or spending without the owner asking. ## Several sessions at once

The owner runs several Claude Code sessions on this project at the same time, from the CLI and from VS Code conversations. Two things keep them apart.

**Sessions in the same folder take turns on a file.** A user-level hook, `~/.claude/hooks/file-queue.mjs` (rules in `../CLAUDE.md`), queues `Edit` and `Write` calls per file. An edit on a file another session is using is refused at once with the place in the queue kept: do other work, then retry, and the retry waits up to 5 minutes for the turn. Read the file again before retrying. The queue does not cover:

- Shell writes. Do not write project files through the shell.
- Subagents of one session, which count as one owner. The orchestrator still gives each its own files.
- The dev port and the git index. One session at a time runs the server on 5173, and one commits.

**A session doing longer independent work takes its own worktree**: a separate checkout at `.claude/worktrees/<name>` on branch `worktree-<name>`, started from the current commit (`worktree.baseRef` in `.claude/settings.json`).

- From a terminal: `claude -w <name>` in the project folder.
- From a VS Code conversation: Claude Code 2.1.322 cannot create a worktree there ("Refusing to use ... as an isolation worktree", because VS Code gives the folder path a lowercase `c:`). Create it with `git worktree add .claude/worktrees/<name> -b worktree-<name>`, then enter it by its path; entering an existing worktree works.
- A worktree needs no `node_modules`. It sits inside the project folder, so Node, `tsc`, `npm run` and Vite find the main checkout's packages by looking upward. **Never link or junction `node_modules` into a worktree**: `git worktree remove` deletes what is behind a junction (tested 2026-11-16 on a dummy folder), and installs are not allowed.
- Ignored files are not in a worktree: `.env.worldlabs` (pass `--env-file=../../../.env.worldlabs`), `.local/`, `dist/`.
- The routes accept only port 5173, so one checkout serves at a time. Stop your server when you have looked.
- To bring work back, commit in the worktree, then run `git merge worktree-<name>` in the main checkout. Remove a finished worktree with `git worktree remove`.

## What exists

`index.html` loads `src/main.tsx`, which mounts `AstralRoom`. There is one page; the only interface drawn over the room is the inspection marks, the replacement menu and the data marks at the frame's four corners (live values as numerals, ticks and bars), none with words.

**What a visitor can do, from start to end (the showcase state, 2026-11-17).** Everything is a recording or a placeholder where this says so; nothing is generated live.

1. **The void.** Black, with EVA's eye. No room exists, so one body of grains waits in the middle and takes a form for each of three moods. The visitor steps between them and picks one.
2. **The tunnel.** A spiral tunnel of points carries the viewer along it, 12 seconds at least, while that mood's own room loads.
3. **The room arrives.** The picked mood's **recorded base room** forms at the tunnel's end out of the tunnel's own points, gathered along ribbons of thin lines, over a 12 second flight: a small office whose desk is sinking into the carpet (quiet), a sitting room whose television has gone soft (uncanny), or a works canteen with one table bent into an arch (eerie). It arrives in its mood's cast.
4. **Looking round.** Keys move the viewer; the mouse's place across the page is where they stand, side to side. If they never move side to side, EVA says so, **in her recorded voice; the wording is placeholder**.
5. **A pick.** Looking at one of the room's four slots (or coming within 1.2 m) brackets it; `F` or a click opens a ring of choices round the eye: the six recorded shelf objects, "nothing", a door and a second eye. The visitor picks what; EVA decides how: plainly, or with the one discrepancy her mood prefers (displaced in the office, repeated or enlarged in the sitting room, always plain in the canteen). What stood there shatters and leaves, and the new thing spirals in, then makes its pose's move.
6. **A refusal.** The door and the second eye are always refused; the grammar refuses a pick that would take away what tells a visitor what the room is, one that does not fit, and "nothing" where nothing stands. EVA says a line, the entry turns red, the menu stays open, nothing changes.
7. **The spent room.** Each foreign thing adds weight. Past 70 the room is spent: every entry of its menu is red and every pick refused.
8. **Leaving.** After 45 seconds without input the room disperses into the void. A room that is not spent is remembered, in this browser, and the next visitor arrives in it as it was left. A spent room is over: its ledger and its mood are forgotten.
9. **The void again**, offering the three moods.

Sequence: black void with the eye. If no room exists, the void offers the three moods first (next paragraph). If one exists, any key press or mouse movement counts as a visitor arriving and starts a spiral tunnel of points, which carries the viewer along it (at least 12 seconds). The room forms at the far end from the tunnel's own points. A 12 second arrival flight follows, then the visitor has control. After 45 seconds in the room with no input, the room disperses back into the void and waits for the next visitor.

**The void's choice (2026-11-17, `LIMINAL-GRAMMAR.md` step 6).** While no room exists, one body of grains waits in the middle of the void and takes an abstract form for the selected mood, with no words: quietly liminal, subtly uncanny, eerie absence, in that order. Stepping to another mood throws the grains and gathers them into its form. Nothing starts until one is picked. **The owner chose this as the final version (2026-11-17).** It is the third round, made after the owner's references (two pictures of glass forms, and bricelab.dev, where one body of particles re-forms for each item). Two earlier rounds were turned down, and their code and captures are deleted: small tinted copies of the library, then three still figures of dots side by side.

- **Whether a room exists** is `mood-choice.ts`: `chosenMood()`, `chooseMood(mood)`, `forgetMood()`. The mood is kept in the browser's storage (key `eva.astral.mood`) and read back as untrusted data: anything but a mood's name is no room. A marked shortcut, as the room's memory is: one machine, one browser profile, one address.
- **Picking** uses the menu's keys and mouse: `A`/`D` or the arrow keys, or mouse travel (a sixth of the page's width for each step, four times the menu's, so the body has time to gather), move between the three; `Enter`, `F` or a click picks. `Esc` does nothing (the choice opens again on the first mood). The pick stores the mood and starts the tunnel into **that mood's own recorded base room** (since 2026-11-17; `loadRoom` passes the mood to `loadRecordedRoomCloud`), and the console says at each pick that the room is a recording.
- **The forms** (`moodForm` in `mood-choice.ts`, pure) are 40,000 grains each, as ordered dots on a surface made in code, every grain with a place in all three. Which form stands for which mood was my proposal, and stands with the owner's choice of this version:
  - Quiet: a sphere parted by a seam that swings round it, one half standing a little proud (the owner's second picture).
  - Uncanny: a flat ribbon in a figure of eight, its upper loop the larger, turned once on its way round (the owner's first picture).
  - Eerie: a ring round nothing, tipped toward the viewer. No reference was given for a third; it was made to sit with the other two.
- **How each moves** (`mood-view.ts`; cut down from the field's effects, which are adapted from Gather): quiet breathes, swelling and sinking in slow uneven lobes; uncanny wrings itself, each height turning its own way and back; eerie is weightless, with regions lifting and three grains in ten leaving the surface, thinning out and forming again. The whole body turns slowly. A change of form is Gather's kick: each grain crosses at its own moment, thrown outward on the way, about a second and a half in all.
- **How it is lit: few colours** (owner, 2026-11-17: "more liminal and more surreal rather than colorful"; the first try had a rainbow toward the edges). Each form is its mood's cast (`MOOD_CASTS`: yellow-green, teal, warm white with green in it) with 45% of its colour kept (`MUTED`), going toward a dull bone white at the edges (`PALE`), the far side showing through dimly. So the three are a dull olive, a pale grey-teal and a bone white, and the eye's red is the only strong colour on screen. Grains are whole-pixel squares of two or three sizes. Three ticks under the body say which of the three it is.
- **Drawn by its own small view** (`createMoodView`), not by the menu's: the menu's view draws separate still choices and cannot move grains between forms. The body stands in the middle of the frame, not round the eye; the eye is left in its idle phase and wanders past it.
- Sizes, speeds, amounts and colours are named at the top of `mood-view.ts` or beside the motion they belong to. They are the values of the chosen version; change them only when the owner asks.
- **Merged with step 5 on 2026-11-17.** Every ending of a room (`endRoom`: a spent room whose visitor has left, the owner's key) calls `forgetMood()`, so the next void offers the three moods. **The mood picked is the room loaded**, so the room's mood, kind, era and starting values are its own manifest's (the shortcut that took the library as any mood's room went on 2026-11-17).

**Object replacement, data side (2026-11-16; the page calls it through the visitor side, below).** `object-points.ts` exports four functions:

- `loadShelf(signal)`: the recorded shelf of six models as `{ id, name, glb }`, with `glb` a URL, each also the grammar's `Thing` (true height in metres, the model's width and depth against its height, the surfaces it stands on, its kinds of place, its year, whether it may be repeated). The console says it is a recording.
- `loadObjectPoints(glbUrl, box, signal, density = 2, height?, stance?)`: a model as points in room coordinates, with normals and unlit texture colour. With a `height` in metres and a box that is a slot, the thing stands in the slot at that height, turned to the slot's facing (`standInSlot`), in the pose `stance` names (`rests` unless given; see Poses and arrivals below), and the result carries the `move` its arrival makes. The model's bounds are now its vertices' own, not its boxes'. Without, it is scaled evenly to the largest size that fits the box, centred, standing on the box's floor: the menu's choices are made this way. Either way every point is 1 mm clear of the box's faces. `density` is linear against the sampler's lattice (2 is half the spacing, four times the points) and is the factor to tune from captures; an object is capped at 250,000 points, past which its whole lattice coarsens.
- `standInSlot(extent, hero, height, how?)`: pure. A thing touches the slot's surface at the slot's point, grows from there along the normal, and is centred on the point the other two ways; `how` gives the air under it and the share of it behind the surface, for a pose. **A thing that would leave the clear volume is made as much smaller as it takes**, and the console says what share of its height it kept: the clear volume is what the room has free there.
- `replaceHero(room, hero, object)`: a new cloud without the room's points inside that hero's box, then the object's points; that hero's `count` becomes the object's points in the box, and everything else is carried over.
- `floorOf`, `litLikeRoom`, `roomCast` (step 4): see Discrepancies below. `loadObjectPoints`'s `stance` also takes `places`, and its result may carry `parts`.

**Slots and true size (2026-11-16, `LIMINAL-GRAMMAR.md` step 2).** A hero of the library that can be changed is a slot: its manifest entry has a `slot` (id, surface, anchor, the point on its surface, the normal, a facing, what the recording holds), and its box, with its numbers unchanged, is the slot's clear volume. The pool has no slot. Normal and facing each lie along an axis; the facing is level. `baseRoom(manifest)` in `posed-frames.ts` gives the grammar's `BaseRoom`: a slot's clear width is across its facing and its clear depth along it. The page shows a thing at its true height times the size EVA gave it (1 unless she chose wrong scale). The shortcut that showed the chess knight at 6 times its height went with step 5, and the shelf now declares the knight as what its model was made as, a garden chess piece 0.6 m tall.

**Poses and arrivals (2026-11-16, `LIMINAL-GRAMMAR.md` step 3). Since step 5 a menu pick stands the thing in the pose EVA decides and plays that pose's arrival. `G` still stands every thing in `rests`; the poses' other ways and `grains` are reached only through the development switch `?swap=`.**

- **The seven poses** (`object-points.ts` `poseIn`, pure; `loadObjectPoints` takes a `stance`). A posture is a turn from upright, metres of air under the thing, and the share of it behind the surface. `rests` and `dropped` stand alike. `hovers` has 0.3 m of air under it (none in an air slot, whose point is already under the thing). `sunk` has 40% of its extent along the slot's normal behind the surface, and its points stop at the surface: the sampler clips the model there, so the cut is clean to within one row of dots. `leans` is tipped back 15 degrees, away from the way the slot faces. `mounted` is upright with its back to the surface, exactly as a thing resting on a wall. `turned` faces the wall behind it, 180 degrees from the slot's facing. Nothing is simulated.
- **Four poses have another way, not chosen** (`POSE_OTHER`; delete the ways the owner does not keep): sunk `tilted` 20 degrees; leans to its `side`; mounted by its `base`, standing on the wall as on a floor; turned to a `corner`, 135 degrees. `grammar-decide.ts` checks the room only of the first way of each.
- **Size.** The thing keeps its true height in every pose; where the pose would take it out of the slot's clear volume it is made smaller, as in step 2, and the console says the share and the pose.
- **The arrivals** (`object-change.ts`: `Move`, `MOVES`, `swapTimes`; the shader's last block in `swapGrain`). `assembles` is the change alone, 5 seconds: the old thing shatters and leaves, the new one spirals in (the owner's pick, 2026-11-17). In `rises`, `tips` and `turns` the thing assembles that way where its move starts from (on the surface, upright, facing the room), and when 90% of that has run it moves into its pose: 3 s easing up, 1.4 s falling into its lean with one small rebound, 3 s turning. `pushes-up` does not assemble: the thing comes whole through the surface over 4 s, cut off at it, starting when 30% of the leaving has run, while the old thing leaves. `falls` is a hovering thing dropped (0.9 s, lands, one small rebound): the page says the same thing stood there (`carried`), so nothing leaves and nothing assembles. Seconds and curves are starting values, constants at the top of `object-change.ts`.
- **A move is a turn about an axis through the middle of the thing, and a shift**, which `loadObjectPoints` works out from the two postures and returns as `move`. The thing's points are stored where it ends, so nothing has to be rebuilt when the change is over. `grains` is a variant, not chosen: each grain makes the move at its own moment (spread over half of it) instead of the thing as one body.
- `field.replaceHero(hero, object, progress, move)` takes the move last; without one a change is the leaving and assembling alone. A change's length is `swapTimes(move).total`, and `?swap=`'s progress is a share of that.

**Discrepancies (2026-11-17, `LIMINAL-GRAMMAR.md` step 4, as changed the same day by the owner's decisions). Since step 5 a menu pick carries whichever of them EVA decides; the development switch `?swap=` reaches each directly, by its seventh part.** Each discrepancy a `Change` can carry shows in the room, default look.

- **Wrong scale** (`x2.5`): the thing at one of `grammar-decide.ts`'s allowed sizes times its true height, standing on its slot's surface. **Larger only** (owner): `SCALE_FACTORS` is 2.5, 4, 6, and the grammar never makes a thing smaller, so a thing that fits its slot only small is refused (`fit`). It is the ordinary placement with another height. **A thing made larger is sampled coarser** (`ENLARGED_DENSITY` in `AstralRoom.tsx`, 1.4, against an object's usual 2 and the room's 1): at 2 it is a smooth picture, more detailed than the room round it (owner); at 1 it is more gap than grain from near and what stands behind shows through it. A starting value.
- **Repetition** (`n3` to `n6`; six at most, owner): the copies are **never side by side**. One stands in the slot picked and each other in one of the room's **places**, in the manifest's order (`loadObjectPoints`'s `places`), at the size the first came out and in its place's own pose. A place (`RoomPlace` in `types.ts`, `places` in the room's manifest, on the cloud as `places`) is a clear volume with a slot that holds nothing, validated as a slot is, with an optional `pose`. The library declares five: against the right bookshelf, floating in the pool (`sunk`: 40% under the water, cut off at it), on the carpet left of the pool, on the carpet at the back right, against the left bookshelf. They were measured from `points.bin` and are the owner's to move. With fewer places than copies asked, fewer stand, and the console says so. The copies share 600,000 points (`COPIES_MAX_POINTS`), each an equal part and none more than one object's 250,000.
- **Displacement** is step 3's poses, unchanged.
- **A slot's carpet is a cloud of its own** (owner, 2026-11-17). The first time a hero that stands on a floor is replaced, by the menu, `G` or the switch, the page lays that slot's floor (`floorOf`) through `field.layFloor(hero, floor)`: static grains marked `SWAP_FLOOR`, which no change takes away, brings or moves, and which a replaced hero's box does not take. It stays until the room is set again, whatever stands, sinks, rises or leaves there. So a replaced thing no longer stands in a black patch. What the ledger has standing in a floor slot gets the same floor when the room arrives, laid the same way. Wall and air slots get none.
- **Absence** (`gone`): the thing is gone and **the floor is clean, with no trace** (owner; the three traces of the first round are deleted). The switch first stands the thing plainly, then takes it away: it leaves by the pairing, nothing arrives, and the laid carpet is all that is left. `floorOf` in `object-points.ts`, pure: nothing of what the hero's box held is kept (a recording has no floor under its own things, and what floor a box does hold is sampled finer than the room's and mixed with the thing's colours). The slot's whole footprint is laid with **a copy of the real floor beside it**, the points as they are, moved across: of the four neighbouring pieces of floor, each the footprint's own size, the one that covers most once points far from the floor's colour are left out. Where the room's own floor remains under the box, nothing is laid over it. It is read from the room **as it arrived** (`arrived` in `AstralRoom.tsx`). The console says how many points were copied and that it is a copy. In the books' slot: 11,266 points over 97% of it; in the statue head's: 7,677.
- **A placed thing is lit as the room is** (`litLikeRoom`, `roomCast` in `object-points.ts`; the menu's thumbnails are not): a shelf model's colours are its bare texture, evenly bright from every side, so it read as pasted in and a pale one (the knight) as a flat cut-out. Its upward faces keep their light, its undersides 35% (`LIT_UNDER`), and its colour goes 70% of the way to the room's cast (`LIT_CAST`), the mean colour of the room's points. Starting values. This applies to every replacement, the menu's and the remembered ones too.
- **The field takes an object's grains as given** (`field.replaceHero(hero, object, ...)`, `Stood` in `object-change.ts`; `heroGrains` is gone): they may stand anywhere in the room, which the copies and the laid floor need. `parts` says which grains are which copy, and each copy leaves and arrives about the middle of its own place (the shader's `aMid`).

**EVA decides (2026-11-17, `LIMINAL-GRAMMAR.md` step 5).** A visitor's pick in the menu goes through `decideChange` (`grammar-decide.ts`), and what it returns is what happens. The wiring is in `AstralRoom.tsx` (`pickMenu`, `standIn`, `loadRoom`, `endRoom`).

- **A pick.** The slot is the inspected hero's, the thing is the shelf id picked, or none for "nothing". The seed is `pickSeed` (`replacements.ts`): the ledger's length times 31 plus the slot's index, never chance, so the same picks in the same order are decided the same way. The time is `Date.now()`. The grammar reads neither itself.
- **Accepted.** The decision's changes are added to the ledger and stored at once, then shown in order, one after the other: the visitor's, then each that puts an old strong discrepancy right. Each stands in its pose, at its size, in its number, and arrives by its pose's arrival (step 3). A `dropped` change is the thing already hovering there, carried down: nothing leaves and nothing assembles. "Nothing" is an absence: the thing leaves and nothing arrives. The menu does not open again until the last change has run.
- **Refused.** Each of the four refusals (`unknown`, `spent`, `fit`, `anchor`) has lines in `tease.ts` (`REFUSALS`), placeholder wording in her recorded voice. The entry turns the eye's red, the menu stays open so the visitor can pick something else, and nothing else changes, as with the door and the second eye. The red entry is the only sign of a refusal that does not depend on her line being heard. The pool, the door and the second eye are refused as before, without asking the grammar.
- **The room's memory is the ledger** (`replacements.ts`: `readLedger`, `writeLedger`, key `eva.astral.ledger`), read back through `parseLedger` as untrusted data. **This is a shortcut, marked `shortcut:`, until step 9: the room is remembered on one machine, in one browser profile, for one address.** The earlier list of replacements (`eva.astral.replacements`) is no longer read or written.
- **A room that arrives with a ledger.** While the recorded room loads, what the ledger has standing in each changed slot (`standingNow`) is made ready, in its pose, size and number. As the room forms, each is put back through the field as an object of its own, settled, on its laid floor, exactly as a change leaves it; so the next change there takes all of it away. Nothing of the ledger is baked into the room's cloud any more.
- **A spent room** (deviation over 70) refuses every further pick aloud, and its menu opens with every entry already red. When that visitor leaves (45 seconds without input) it ends: the ledger is cleared, and the next visitor arrives in the recording. A spent ledger found when a room loads (the page went before the 45 seconds ran out) is ended the same way before the room is shown. Every ending goes through `endRoom`, which also forgets the room's mood (`forgetMood()`, step 6), so "arrives in the recording" here now means: is offered the three moods, and then arrives in the recording.
- **The owner's key, `Shift` + `Delete`, in any build**, ends the room by hand at any stage: whatever is formed disperses and the ledger is cleared. Recommended and built, for the owner to overturn; it is one line in `onKeyDown`.
- **Development server only:** after each decision the console has what EVA decided, the three values, the state, and whether the room is spent. A visitor never sees them.
- **What EVA chooses follows the room's mood**, which is the mood picked in the void, since that picks the room. `quiet` prefers displacement and light, and light is not a thing's to carry, so there she only displaces. Wrong scale and repetition come only from `uncanny`. In `eerie` a picked thing always arrives plainly. The step 5 captures of wrong scale and repetition were made before the merge, by answering the manifest with the `uncanny` mood in the capture's own browser (`capture-uncanny.mjs`).
- **The grammar counts the room's places** (`BaseRoom.places`, set by `baseRoom` from the manifest's list): a repeated thing never has more copies than one more than the room has places, and a room with fewer than two places repeats nothing. A place is not asked whether the thing belongs on its surface: places are written by hand for what should stand there.
- A room the grammar does not know (`?room=surreal`, which declares no room) keeps no ledger: a pick there stands the thing plainly and nothing is remembered.

The live route `/api/object` (below) makes a fresh model of one of the same six objects. **Today it refuses every real model at its last step**; see Known faults.

**Object replacement, visitor side (2026-11-16; what a pick does is EVA's decision since step 5, above).** With keyboard and mouse only, a visitor looks at a hero, opens a menu, picks a replacement and watches the old object leave and the new one arrive. Everything offered is the recorded shelf; nothing is generated live.

- **The menu** (`menu.ts`, `menu-view.ts`). While a hero is inspected, `F` or a click opens it. Each choice is the object itself as a small point cloud, turning slowly; there are no words. After the six objects comes **nothing**, drawn as the bare edges of an empty box in dots (`entryPoints` in `menu.ts`; its look is a first try, not judged), then the two refused entries. The selected one is the largest and brightest, in corner brackets. The rest of the room steps back to about a third of its light. While it is open the eye is put in its "thinking" phase, which brings it in front of the viewer, and the choices come out of the eye and stay round wherever it is on screen (moved in from the frame's edge so they all fit). `A`/`D` or the arrow keys move one choice along and wrap round; the mouse moves one choice for every 1/24 of the page's width it travels, and the side-to-side view holds still meanwhile. `Enter`, `F` or a click picks; `Esc` closes. The flight never sees the keys while the menu is open. The menu does not open during a change, or while any of the painted room is on screen.
- **The choices sit in a ring** (the owner's pick of three arrangements, 2026-11-17; the row and the one-at-a-time are deleted): they circle the eye, and the circle turns to bring the selected one below it.
- **The change** (`object-change.ts`, `point-field.ts`). The page stands the picked model in the hero's slot as EVA decided and calls `replaceHero`; the field draws its points as an object of its own beside the room's grains, which are never rebuilt, so every other grain keeps its place and its index. What stood in the box **shatters and leaves** (it cracks into chunks, each tumbling as it goes and falling) and the new points **spiral in**, wheeling round the middle, over 5 seconds: the owner's pick of three pairings, 2026-11-17. A hero can be replaced again: then the earlier replacement is what leaves.
- **Pushback** (`tease.ts`, `REFUSALS`). The last two entries are requests EVA will not do: a door, and a second eye. Picking one makes her say a line (in her recorded voice), the entry turns the eye's red, and nothing changes. She also refuses, with a line and no menu, to have the pool replaced, because its water is simulated apart from the points. The wording is placeholder.
- **Memory** is the room's ledger since step 5; see EVA decides, above.

`/api/object` (`src/server/object.ts`) takes `POST { "id": ... }` where the id is one of six objects in a fixed list in that file (`OBJECTS`: diving helmet, gramophone, rocking horse, globe, chess knight, televisions); the prompt is built on the server and no visitor text reaches a provider. It asks OpenAI for a 1536x1024 picture, sends it to Meshy for a textured model (smart topology, 10,000 target faces), polls every 2 s, downloads the GLB only from `assets.meshy.ai` (8 MiB at most), checks it with `validatePropGlb` and replies with the GLB bytes (`model/gltf-binary`); a failure is JSON with an `error` code. One request at a time, six minutes at most. `GET` also lists the six ids. On success the server console logs the timings, the picture's token count and the model's credits as live generation.
